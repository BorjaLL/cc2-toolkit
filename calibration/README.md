# CC2 calibration plan (per filament)

Written 2026-09-21. Printer: Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2. Slicer: ElegooSlicer
1.5.3 (an OrcaSlicer fork, so the OrcaSlicer wiki applies). Spool-agnostic:
run it once per filament brand + type, starting from that material's stock base
preset (e.g. `Elegoo PLA @ECC2` for plain PLA). Builds on `guides/tuning.md`;
read that for the why. Results live in `filaments/<slug>.md` (see
`filaments/README.md`), never in this file.

**Total: 4 prints, about 1.75 h of printing plus ~30 min of looking.** Do it in
one sitting or one test per evening. Each step stands alone.

**Shortcut: the calibration card** (`calibration/card/`, one print, ~47 min
with its temperature bracket, printed after the card as its own object with a
temperature wait per band, or ~27 min without). It brackets values you
already roughly know: 7 flow pads (+-0.03), 5 retraction pairs (0.4-1.2 mm),
the 6 tolerance holes + tester, and a +-10 C mini tower, all labelled on the
part. Use it for a new spool of a known material or a re-check; use Steps 1-4
below for an unknown material or when the card says a value is off its scale.
Its README says what it can and cannot replace.

## The sequence (do in this order)

| # | Test | Why this order | Print time (approx) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Pre-flight | clean plate + good first layer, or every result lies | 10 min |
| 1 | Temperature | everything else depends on temp | 55 min (sliced estimate) |
| 2 | Flow ratio | needs the right temp first | 20 min |
| 3 | Retraction | stringing depends on temp + flow | 20 min |
| 4 | Tolerance | needs the right flow; matters for sliding fits | ~12 min (measured) |

**SKIP the slicer tests for Pressure advance and Input shaping.**

- Input shaping: the printer measures and compensates vibration itself during
  its full calibration. Nothing to do in the slicer.
- Pressure advance: every stock preset ships its own PA and sends it to the
  printer (plain PLA and PLA+ 0.04, PLA PRO and PLA-CF 0.032, PETG 0.05,
  PETG PRO 0.1). Elegoo says the printer's full calibration also tunes PA.
  Which value wins is unverified. Leave the selected preset's stock PA
  alone, do not run the slicer PA test, and revisit only if corners bulge or
  seams gap after Steps 1-4.

If you ever swap the nozzle or hotend, re-run the printer's full calibration
from its touchscreen (Settings), then Steps 1-3 here.

Optional extras (not core, only if a specific problem shows up later):
Max flowrate (if fast prints under-extrude) and Cornering (if corners bulge).
Two lines on each at the bottom.

Steps 1-3 live in the top menu bar: **Calibration**. Step 4 is a handy model
you add to the plate. The Calibration tests are generated by the GUI (the
slicer varies a setting band by band), so the headless slicer
in `tools/` cannot make them. One exception already exists: a
dedicated generator and a ready-to-print temperature tower for plain Elegoo PLA
in `calibration/temperature/` (see Step 1).

## Step 0: Pre-flight (10 min, once per session)

1. **Door as you normally print.** Door shut is fine for PLA on the CC2: the
   cavity fan holds the chamber at 34-36 C (see `guides/tuning.md`).
   Calibrate in the same condition you will print in.
2. **Clean the plate.** Textured PEI side up. Warm water + a drop of dish soap,
   rinse, dry, then a wipe of IPA. Handle by the edges.
3. **In ElegooSlicer**, Prepare tab: printer `Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 0.4
   nozzle`, filament = the stock base preset for your material (e.g. `Elegoo
   PLA @ECC2`), process `0.20mm Standard @Elegoo CC2
   0.4 nozzle`, plate type **Textured PEI Plate** (the dropdown next to the printer; if it says
   Cool Plate the bed will be 35C and nothing will stick).
4. **First layer check** on the first test print. The CC2 auto-levels before
   each print. Watch the first layer for 1-2 minutes:
   - Good: lines are flat, slightly squashed, touching each other, no gaps.
   - Too high: lines are round, do not touch, peel off. Move nozzle CLOSER.
   - Too low: lines are see-through, rough, nozzle scrapes. Move nozzle AWAY.
   Adjust on the printer touchscreen (Settings > Z offset / Z-axis deviation)
   in the smallest step offered, ideally 0.05 mm, then watch 30 s before the
   next step. Elegoo's guide:
   https://wiki.elegoo.com/Centauri-carbon/tutorial-for-fine-tuning-after-automatic-re-leveling
5. **Record the Z offset** you ended on in your own notes (it is a printer
   fact, not a filament one).
   If the first layer is good in one spot and bad in another, re-run the
   printer's bed mesh, do not chase it with the global offset.

## Step 0b: Objective Z-offset / first-layer test (optional, 5 min)

Eyeballing the live first layer (Step 0 item 4) works, but a single-layer patch
is repeatable and honest. ElegooSlicer has **no built-in** Z/first-layer test
(confirmed, tagged wontfix upstream), so you print a patch and read it.

**Ready to print:** `calibration/zoffset/CC2_ZoffsetTest_FirstLayer_PLA.gcode`
- 80x80 mm, exactly one 0.2 mm layer, ~5 min. Sliced for the calibrated emoji
  PLA (210 C, flow 1.00, see `filaments/elegoo-pla-emoji.md`).
- Regenerate: `python calibration/zoffset/gen_zoffset.py out.stl`, then
  `python tools/slice_cc2.py out.stl --filament <slicer_key> --temp <temp> --flow <flow>`
  with the values from the loaded filament's `filaments/<slug>.md`.

**Read the patch (hold it to a light):**
- **Correct:** one fused sheet, faint even PEI texture, no gaps, no ridges; peels off
  flexible, not brittle.
- **Too high** (nozzle far): gaps/valleys between lines, ribbed, brittle -> **lower Z**
  (more negative).
- **Too low** (nozzle close): glassy/translucent, over-squished, drag ripples, elephant
  foot bulge -> **raise Z**.

**Adjust on the CC2 (what owners actually do):**
- Set Z on the **touchscreen while a print is running** (baby-step). The value saves
  across power cycles.
- Direction: **positive = nozzle further from bed (up); negative = closer (down)**.
- Steps 0.02-0.05 mm. Typical landing 0.03-0.08. If you need -0.15 or more, stop:
  clean the plate (dish soap + water, then IPA) and re-run the **full startup
  calibration** - that fixes most "Z is way off" cases.

**CC2-specific gotchas (from r/elegoo owner threads, Sep-Nov 2025):**
1. **Leveling RESETS the Z-offset.** Turn OFF "heated bed leveling / level before each
   print" in the slicer so your Z persists; you should not need to level every print.
2. The slicer Z-offset (Printer settings) is **relative** - it ADDS to the device
   value. Set it in ONE place, not both, or you double up.
3. Save a separate Z per **plate side** (smooth vs textured differ in thickness).
4. Known **warped-bed** batch issue (high middle, low corners). If corners won't tram
   in, it may be the bed, not you - there's a print-in-place leveling tool
   (printables.com/model/1392124) and Elegoo has replaced beds.
5. Wide/bulged first layer = **elephant foot**: raise EFC to 0.1-0.2, or lower bed temp,
   or your Z is too low / flow too high. Calibrating flow (Step 2) alone fixed it for
   several owners.

Sources: r/elegoo Centauri Carbon Z-offset & first-layer threads (2025); OrcaSlicer
wiki; obico & uavmodel first-layer guides.

## Step 1: Temperature tower (55 min)

Wiki: https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/wiki/temp_calib

**Shortcut for plain PLA:** a tower is already sliced with the plain Elegoo PLA
stock values: `calibration/temperature/CC2_Emoji_PLA_TempTower_230-190C_0.4mm.gcode`
(230 to 190 C, 5 C per block, 55 min, textured PEI). It uses **CANVAS slot 1 /
T0**: check the filament you want to test is actually in slot 1 first (see
`filaments/README.md`). The README next to it has the details and
`build_tower.py` regenerates it. Copy it to USB, print, and jump to item 2.
Otherwise:

1. **Launch:** Calibration > Temperature. The dialog always opens with the
   PLA range (start 230, end 190, step 5) no matter which preset is loaded.
   For PLA accept it. For any other material click that material's radio
   button first; the preset alone does not change the range. Click OK,
   slice, print.
2. **Look at** each block (each is labelled with its temperature on the side):
   - Stringing between the two posts: less is better.
   - Overhang and bridge: no sagging, no curled edges.
   - Side walls: smooth and shiny, not matte/rough, not dripping.
   - Layer bond: try to flex the tower; a block that cracks between layers is
     too cold.
3. **Pick** the block that looks best overall. If 2-3 neighbouring blocks all
   look good, take the **middle** one. Prefer the hotter end of that range if
   you print fast (the CC2 does).
4. **Record:** `Temp = ___ C` in `filaments/<slug>.md`. Stock for Elegoo
   PLA is 210; plain PLA usually lands 200-215.
5. **Save:** Filament dropdown > edit (pencil) > Filament tab > Print
   temperature > Nozzle: set **Other layers** to your value and **Initial
   layer** to the same or +5. Click the save icon > name it
   `<Brand Material> (calibrated)` (e.g. `Elegoo PLA emoji (calibrated)`) >
   Save. Use this preset from now on.

## Step 2: Flow ratio (20 min)

Wiki: https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/wiki/flow_ratio_calib

1. **Launch:** Calibration > Flow ratio. In the dialog pick **YOLO
   (Recommended)**. Make sure the calibrated preset from Step 1 is selected
   first. Slice, print.
2. **Look at** the 11 square patches, labelled -0.05 to +0.05. Hold each up to
   the light at an angle:
   - Good: top surface is smooth and even, arcs blend into each other, no
     visible line between the inner spiral and the outer arcs.
   - Too little flow (negative side too far): gaps between the arcs, you can
     see the layer below.
   - Too much flow (positive side too far): ridges, bumpy, rough to the
     fingernail.
   Run a fingernail across each patch. The smoothest one wins.
3. **Pick** the smoothest patch. Ties: pick the one nearer 0.
4. **Record:** new flow = old flow + patch label. Stock for Elegoo PLA is 0.98.
   Example: patch `+0.01` wins -> `0.98 + 0.01 = 0.99`. `Flow = ___`.
5. **Save:** edit the calibrated preset > Filament tab > Flow ratio and
   Pressure Advance > **Flow ratio** = your number > save icon. Leave
   Pressure advance at the preset's stock value (0.04 for plain PLA; see the
   skip note at the top).

If two patches look identical and you care: same dialog, **YOLO
(Perfectionist)** is the same test in 0.005 steps. Skip for PLA.

## Step 3: Retraction (20 min)

Wiki: https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/wiki/retraction_calib

1. **Launch:** Calibration > Retraction. Defaults are right for the CC2
   (direct drive): start 0 mm, end 2 mm, step 0.1 mm. Slice, print.
2. **Look at** the tower. Retraction increases as it goes up. Find the lowest
   height from which the strings between the two posts are gone (or thin
   wisps you can blow off). Going higher does not help and costs time.
   Measure that height from the plate with calipers or a ruler.
3. **Read the length from the slicer, not from the ruler.** In the sliced
   preview, drag the layer slider to that height and read the G-code comment
   for that layer, `Calib_Retraction_tower: ... length:`. That number is the
   answer. (The slicer steps the length once per mm of height above the
   first 0.4 mm and rounds down, so 8 mm on the ruler is 0.7 mm, not 0.8.
   Trust the comment.)
4. **Record:** `Retraction = ___ mm`. Stock CC2 is 0.8 mm at 30 mm/s. Expect
   0.5-1.2. Do not go above 2 mm on a direct-drive head.
5. **Save:** edit the calibrated preset > **Setting Overrides** tab >
   Retraction > tick **Length** > enter the value > save icon. Saving it in
   the filament preset keeps it per spool and leaves the printer preset alone.
   Leave retraction speed at stock.

## Step 4: Tolerance (~12 min) - this one de-risks sliding-fit prints

Wiki: https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/wiki/tolerance_calib

The author's Coal Train puzzle (a train with sliding-fit wagons) is the reason for this step.
This test tells you how much gap the CC2 needs for a part to slide, before you
print a 3-plate train and find the wagons are welded together.

1. **Launch:** right-click the empty plate > Add Handy Model > **Orca
   Tolerance Test**. (This one has no Calibration menu entry.) Use the
   calibrated preset. Slice, print.
2. **Look at** the six hexagonal holes, marked 0.0 / 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3 /
   0.4 mm of extra gap. Push the printed hex tester into each hole by hand,
   no tools, no force. Then repeat with a 6 mm Allen key; the two differ, so
   note both.
3. **Pick** the **smallest** gap where the printed tester slides fully in and
   out freely. That is a result for this coupon and this tester, not a number
   you can paste onto any model.
4. **Measure** with calipers, across the flats. The labels are PER SIDE: the
   tester is 6.0 mm nominal and the holes are 6.0 / 6.1 / 6.2 / 6.4 / 6.6 /
   6.8 mm for labels 0 / 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3 / 0.4 (measured from the
   bundled model). Measure the tester and the 0.2 hole, then work out each
   error on its own:
   - contour error per side = (tester measured - 6.0) / 2
   - hole error per side = (6.4 - 0.2 hole measured) / 2
   Example: tester 6.06 and hole 6.30 -> contour +0.03, hole shrink 0.05.
   **Record:** `Free at = ___; tester = ___ mm; 0.2 hole = ___ mm`. A
   well-tuned CC2 is free at 0.1-0.2 with both errors under 0.05. If even
   0.4 is tight, measure before blaming anything: it can be flow, elephant
   foot on the first layers, or compensation already set in the process
   preset.
5. **Apply** for fit-critical prints only (a sliding puzzle, a dovetail), and always on the real mating parts first: print ONE wagon and
   its coupling, test the slide, then decide.
   - Slides freely: print the rest as-is.
   - Tight: in the **process** preset, Quality tab > Precision. The two
     settings are separate and each moves its own boundary by the value per
     side, so feed each error only into its own setting: **X-Y contour
     compensation** = minus the contour error (e.g. -0.03) and **X-Y hole
     compensation** = plus the hole error (e.g. +0.05). Do not put the whole
     gap into both; that overcorrects. The author's dovetails used -0.1 mm
     contour. Reprint the one wagon, test again.

## Save it in two places

1. **In the slicer:** every value above goes into the user filament preset
   `<Brand Material> (calibrated)`. It lands on disk at
   `%APPDATA%\ElegooSlicer\user\default\filament\`. Copy the .json into this
   folder tree as `filaments/presets/<slug>.json` when done.
2. **In this repo:** record the numbers in the frontmatter of
   `filaments/<slug>.md` (create it from an existing one if new) and add a
   dated log line saying what you saw and why you picked it. The headless
   slicer in `tools/` does not read that file; pass the values
   explicitly (`--filament <slicer_key> --temp <temp> --flow <flow>`).

Results live in `filaments/<slug>.md` (see `filaments/README.md`), not here.

## New filament? Repeat.

Per filament **brand + type**, not per colour of the same line:

- **New brand or type** (white PLA if it is not Elegoo, the PLA-CF, any
  PLA+/PETG later): start from **that material's stock base preset**, never
  from the calibrated PLA one. It carries the right temperature range,
  cooling and bed temperature, and the Temperature dialog pre-fills a
  different range per material. Bases: plain PLA of any brand `Elegoo PLA
  @ECC2`; PLA-CF `Elegoo PLA-CF @ECC2`; PETG `Elegoo PETG @ECC2` (or `PETG
  PRO`). Save it under its own name, e.g. `Elegoo PLA-CF (calibrated)`. Then
  run Steps 1-3. Step 4 only if flow moved by more than 0.02 or the print is
  fit-critical.
- **Same line, new colour** (another Elegoo PLA RFID spool): reuse the preset.
  Re-run Step 2 (20 min) only if top surfaces look off.
- **Nozzle or hotend swap:** re-run the printer's own on-screen calibration
  (input shaping, plus whatever PA pass it does; see the note at the top),
  then Steps 1-3.
- **PLA-CF specifics:** hardened nozzle only (the stock CC2 nozzle is), door
  can stay open, expect 0.98-1.02 flow and a hotter tower pick (210-230).

## If a test looks wrong

- **Nothing sticks / first layer peels:** Step 0 again. Plate clean? Textured
  PEI selected in the slicer? Z offset one step closer.
- **Every block of a tower looks equally bad:** check the basics before
  reading anything into it: door the same as usual, plate type, calibrated preset actually
  selected (the dropdown resets when you change printers), filament dry. Fix
  what you find and print the tower again.
- **Results shift between prints, or you hear popping / see steam:** filament
  may be damp. Dry it first (PLA about 50C for 4-6 h; Elegoo says 60C for 8 h
  for PETG Pro), then redo Step 1 and Step 3, the two moisture skews most,
  and Step 2 if the top surfaces changed.
- **Do not judge tuning by a Benchy.** It is a torture test. Print one AFTER
  Steps 1-4 as a before/after souvenir, not as a diagnostic.

## Optional extras (only when a symptom appears)

- **Max flowrate** (Calibration > Max flowrate): find the speed at which
  extrusion starts to fail. Only if fast prints show gaps or thin walls. Stock
  preset already caps PLA at 21 mm3/s. Wiki:
  https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/wiki/volumetric_speed_calib
- **Cornering** (Calibration > Cornering): jerk / junction deviation. Only if
  corners bulge or ring on a mass-loaded bench. Wiki:
  https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/wiki/cornering_calib
